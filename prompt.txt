# Encyclo — Design Spec

**Encyclo** is a typing roguelike played on Wikipedia. Each level drops the player into a randomly selected article with an 80-second timer, during which they must navigate via in-article hyperlinks and lock in exactly three full sentences by typing them. Sentence selection and entry are unified through a find-in-page mechanic: as the player types, matching sentences highlight yellow with their full extent visible (showing where the commitment ends), invalid input turns the input box red, Enter on red clears the input, and Enter on a complete match locks the sentence green. Backspace returns to the last valid prefix. Formatting is part of character matching—a plain `s` and a bold `**s**` are different characters—so the player must produce bold (Ctrl+B), italic (Ctrl+I), link (Ctrl+L), citation (Ctrl+C), and the rare sub/superscript states (Ctrl+Shift+,/.) while typing. Special characters use a tiered input scheme: ASCII baseline, compose key for European diacritics, script modes (Alt+G for Greek, etc.) as learnable skills, and a character palette as a slow fallback for true exotica. Frequency analysis of a 32K-character sample confirms 99.5% ASCII coverage, with citations (0.78%) and en-dashes (0.05%) as the most design-load-bearing non-letter characters.

Scoring cascades in three phases—**Word → Sentence → Level**—and must beat a Balatro-style ante threshold to advance. Each phase has three upgrade slots, but multiplier upgrades occupy two slots, capping the player at one mult per phase and forcing build identity at every tier of the cascade. Flat bonuses (per-letter, per-formatting-state, per-digit, per-citation) compete with defensive utility upgrades (typo allowance, auto-formatting, time extensions, article rerolls) for remaining slots. Article variance is intentional and structural: most rolls give every build a workable run, while specialist articles (linguistics, mathematics, foreign-language histories) brick generic builds and reward the narrow upgrade specializations that make them viable. The shop economy must give players answers across multiple difficulty axes—threshold scaling, article scaling, per-ante constraint blinds, and Boss Blind variants—so that the meta-game becomes "what kind of sentence am I hunting" rather than "what numbers am I stacking." The compounding skill curve outside the run economy (script-mode fluency, formatting muscle memory, sentence-pattern recognition across Wikipedia genres) gives Encyclo its long-tail mastery loop.
